Module 1 · 7:05 min

Materials

Why district heating pipe is made of steel, and what that means for design.

After this module you can

  • Name the main values in the stress-strain diagram and how they relate to elastic and plastic deformation
  • Understand how temperature changes the mechanical properties of steel
  • Explain what the designation P235GH means and why the steel welds well
  • Know how fatigue is assessed in district heating

Key points

  • Elastic deformation is reversible; beyond the yield strength a permanent deformation remains
  • The yield strength is the basis of all permissible stresses
  • Steel grows about 0.8 in per 100 ft for every 100 °F (1 mm per m for 80 K)
  • P235GH: minimum yield strength 34.1 ksi (235 N/mm²), carbon below 0.16 %, standard pipe is longitudinally welded
  • PEX expands about 17 times more than steel but is over 200 times softer: restrained, it builds up hardly any force
  • Fatigue is counted in equivalent full load cycles; in practice corrosion and aging of the bonded system limit service life more often

Knowledge check

1. What does “235” in P235GH stand for?

235 is the minimum yield strength in N/mm² at room temperature, about 34.1 ksi.

2. Which value is the basis for the permissible stresses in pipe statics?

The yield strength is the limit between elastic and plastic behavior. Permissible stresses are derived from it.

3. How much does one meter of steel grow when it is heated by 80 K (144 °F)?

α = 12 × 10⁻⁶ per K: 1 m × 80 K gives about 1 mm.

4. Why is the carbon content of P235GH kept below about 0.16 %?

Low carbon keeps the steel weldable; the weld then has largely the same properties as the base material.

5. Why does a restrained PEX pipe build up far less force than a steel pipe?

σ = E × α × ΔT. PEX expands about 17 times more, but E is 800 against 210,000 N/mm², so the stress stays small.

Module 2 · 10:54 min

Pipe statics: fundamentals

How a buried bonded pipe moves, and the limits that keep it safe.

After this module you can

  • Explain why a buried bonded pipe needs pipe statics
  • Know the three permissible stresses in steel, foam and bond
  • Know the cause of adhesion and sliding zones
  • Use the terms natural anchor point, Lmax and Hmax
  • Know what expansion elements and expansion pads do

Key points

  • Three limits: 27.6 ksi (190 N/mm²) in the steel, 21.7 psi (0.15 N/mm²) in the foam, 5.8 psi (0.04 N/mm²) in the bond
  • Restrained expansion becomes stress: σ = E × α × ΔT. At 188 °F (87 °C) a fully restrained pipe reaches the steel limit
  • Below 188 °F (87 °C), Lmax need not be checked
  • Friction adds up from the free end: sliding zone, then adhesion zone, with the natural anchor point in the middle
  • Expansion is taken up in L, Z and U elements; pads give the pipe room to move. One layer absorbs 1 in (25 mm)
  • Bedding with too much friction tears the foam between steel and jacket: never change the bedding material without the pipe supplier

Knowledge check

1. What is the permissible axial stress in the steel service pipe?

235 N/mm² yield strength, reduced to 210 for temperature and divided by 1.1, gives 190 N/mm².

2. Below which operating temperature can the maximum installation length be ignored?

At ΔT = 77 K from 50 °F (10 °C), a fully restrained pipe just reaches 190 N/mm². Below that, temperature alone cannot overstress it.

3. Where on a straight buried run is the movement largest?

Axial force is lowest and movement largest at the free ends. At the natural anchor point there is no movement.

4. What is the risk of too much cover?

More cover means more friction on the jacket. Hmax keeps the shear stress in the bond at or below 0.04 N/mm². Too little cover is the buckling risk.

5. How much movement does one layer of expansion pad absorb?

One 1.6 in (40 mm) layer absorbs up to about 1 in (25 mm). Up to 3 mm no pads are needed.

6. What does bedding material with too much friction do to a bonded pipe?

The bond can carry 0.04 N/mm² (5.8 psi) in shear. Beyond that the foam tears between steel pipe and jacket.

Module 3 · 11:20 min

Pipe statics: applications

Branches, flat bends, prestressing, twin pipe and bedding.

After this module you can

  • Apply the rules for service connections and tees
  • Judge changes of direction that are not 90°
  • Explain how thermal prestressing works and when it helps
  • Know what is different about twin pipe and which DUO fittings exist
  • Know what to watch with vents and hot tapping
  • Know the requirements for bedding material

Key points

  • 45° tee: direct connection up to 19.5 ft (6 m), otherwise add a Z with about 6.5 ft (2 m) offset
  • Flat bends between long legs overload the steel: place them close to an expansion element or use curved pipe
  • Thermal prestressing halves the temperature difference that creates stress
  • DUO: plates tie supply and return together; the permissible installation length is shorter
  • DUO fittings: bends, 90° tee only, valves under one street cover, Y-pieces type G (straight) and W (angled)
  • Elastic bending: minimum radius about 1,000 × steel diameter; NPS 2 takes 22.5° over two pipes, NPS 10 only 5°
  • Vents: operate as high as possible; use a combined valve with stubs when the depth is not fixed
  • Hot tapping: an opening concentrates stress; it is strengthened with a reinforcement plate
  • Bedding: round, washed sand 0 to 5/16 in (0 to 8 mm); clarify flowable fill with the pipe supplier

Knowledge check

1. How long may a direct service connection from a 45° tee be?

Up to 6 m to the next change of direction or expansion element. Beyond that, add a Z.

2. Which single bend between two long legs loads the steel most?

The flatter the bend, the larger the sideways movement. In the course example a 20° bend reaches 224 % utilization.

3. What does thermal prestressing change?

The pipe is backfilled hot, at about half the operating temperature, so the stress-relevant ΔT is roughly halved.

4. Why is the permissible installation length of DUO pipe shorter than that of single pipe?

Supply and return want to expand differently; the plates force them together and that costs part of the stress reserve.

5. Which bedding material does SN EN 13941-2 ask for?

Round, washed, grain size 0 to 8 mm (0 to 5/16 in). No crushed material.

6. Which branch fittings exist for PREMANT DUO twin pipe?

The 90° tee with static spacers is the only DUO branch. There is no 45° tee and no parallel tee.

7. The final depth of a valve is not known when ordering. Which combined valve do you choose?

A combined valve with factory vents is not suitable for modification on site. With stubs, the vent height is set on site.

Module 4 · 7:06 min

Flexible pipes and thermal insulation

CASAFLEX, CALPEX and the polyurethane foam that insulates them all.

After this module you can

  • Know the two flexible systems and where they are used
  • Know the transition from rigid to flexible pipe
  • Explain how PUR foam is made and why it insulates
  • Know how the foam ages and what protects it

Key points

  • Flexible pipes come in coils and need no pipe statics
  • CASAFLEX: corrugated stainless steel service pipe for high temperature and pressure; CALPEX: PE-Xa service pipe for networks up to 176 °F (80 °C) continuous
  • At the transition to rigid pipe, a tee with a 6.5 ft (2 m) branch acts as expansion leg
  • PUR foam: polyol plus isocyanate, expanded 20 to 30 times with cyclopentane; closed cells
  • 60 to 70 % of the heat crosses the foam through the cell gas. The jacket protects the foam from water and oxygen
  • Prefabricated T-sections and chambers (HYDRA) save cost, time and trench space on site

Knowledge check

1. Which of the three systems needs pipe statics?

Only the rigid steel system. Flexible pipes take up their own expansion; statics matters only at the transition to rigid pipe.

2. Through which path does most heat cross PUR foam?

60 to 70 % through the cell gas, 20 to 25 % through the solid matrix, 15 to 20 % by radiation, no convection.

3. Which blowing agent is used for district heating pipe today?

Cyclopentane: no ozone depletion potential, a GWP of about 11, and it stays in the cells for a very long time.

4. What protects the foam from water and oxygen?

The jacket keeps water and oxygen away from the foam; the monitoring wires report moisture early.

5. How is a flexible pipe connected to a prefabricated BRUGG T-section or chamber?

The rigid part is welded to the main line; the flexible pipe is mounted on the attached fitting without welding.

Module 5 · 7:15 min

Civil works, welding and joints

From route planning to the backfilled trench. Followed by the PREMANT installation video.

After this module you can

  • Plan a simple route and know its rules and limits
  • Know the sequence of construction in an open trench
  • Know the working space and distances in the trench
  • Know how welds are tested and how the line is leak and pressure tested
  • Explain how a joint is sealed and insulated, and how the trench is backfilled

Key points

  • About 60 % of the pipeline construction cost is civil works; keep the route short and use clear 90° offsets
  • Distances between jackets and to the wall: 6, 10 or 12 in (150, 250, 300 mm) depending on jacket size; working pit at joints about 5 ft (1.5 m) long
  • Supports: sandbags or polystyrene blocks, never timber
  • Welds: visual test on all, radiography on 5 to 20 % of a lot, 100 % if defects are found
  • Water test: 1.3 × design pressure for 1 h (strength), 1.1 × for 8 h (tightness)
  • Joint: clean, dry, abrade; connect and test the monitoring wires; seal (butyl and shrink, or INDUCON welding); foam
  • Pipe zone: at least 4 in (10 cm) of washed sand, no crushed material, supports removed
  • Swiss and European rules are the source; local codes and safety rules apply on site

On site: PREMANT installation video (13:29)

The ten installation steps from unloading to final checks. Same video as on the PREMANT product page.

Knowledge check

1. Roughly what share of pipeline construction cost is civil works?

About 60 %. That is why short routes, narrow trenches and fast installation matter so much.

2. Which temporary pipe supports are allowed in the trench?

Sandbags or polystyrene blocks. Timber is not permitted.

3. How is the water pressure test carried out?

1.3 × for one hour proves strength; 1.1 × for eight hours proves tightness (two hours if everything is visible).

4. What share of welds is tested by radiography?

Depending on the project class, 5 to 20 % of a lot. Every weld gets a visual test.

5. Why does the joint casing stay in its weather film until it is installed?

The casing is a shrink part. Sunlight could shrink it before it is in place.
